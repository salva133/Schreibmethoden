# Productivity & Writing Routine

---

## Freewriting

Write for a set time (10-20 minutes) without a break, without correcting, without judging. Everything that comes into your head, even nonsense.

**Goals:**
- Switch off the inner critic
- Get the writing flowing
- Bring unconscious ideas into the light
- Break through writer's block

**Rules:** Do not read back, do not correct, do not stop. When the ideas run out, write "I don't know what to write" until the next image arrives.

**Source:** Peter Elbow, *Writing Without Teachers* (1973), who established freewriting as a writing method.

---

## The Pomodoro Technique
*Developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s*

Split the work into focused blocks:

1. **25 minutes** of concentrated writing (one "pomodoro")
2. **5 minutes** break
3. After 4 pomodoros: a longer break of **15-30 minutes**

**Advantages for authors:**
- Beats procrastination through small, manageable units
- Produces measurable progress
- Prevents mental exhaustion
- A clear end makes the start easier

**Combination:** pomodoro plus a word target per block is especially effective, for instance 500 words per pomodoro.

**Source:** Francesco Cirillo, *The Pomodoro Technique* (2006/2018)

## Stephen King's Daily Routine

- Write **2,000 words** every day, no more, no less.
- Write **daily**, inspiration or not.
- Work starts at 8:00 to 8:30, with tea and music.
- First draft of a novel: **three months at most**.
- **"Write with the door closed, rewrite with the door open."**

**Source:** Stephen King, *On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft* (2000)

---

## Fixed Writing Time & Place

- Pick a **fixed place** used for writing only. The brain ties place to creativity.
- Set a **fixed time of day**: in the morning before the day starts, in the evening after work.
- **Eliminate distractions actively:** phone silent, browser closed, door shut.
- Always use the **same rituals** to get in: make tea, read the last paragraph aloud for five minutes. The brain then switches into writing mode faster.

## Setting Writing Goals

| Method | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Word target per day** | 500 to 2,000 words, depending on the time available |
| **Chapter target** | one chapter a week |
| **Time target** | 30 to 60 minutes of focused writing daily |
| **NaNoWriMo principle** | 50,000 words in 30 days (November), about 1,667 a day |

**Important:** Plan for bad days. A weekly target averaged out is more sustainable than a daily obligation.

## Handling Writer's Block

- **Keep writing, even when it is bad.** The first draft is allowed to be miserable.
- **Change scene.** There is no obligation to write in order.
- **Ask the scene question:** what does my character *really* want in this scene?
- **Letter from the character.** Write yourself a letter in the character's voice.
- **Freewriting** about the blockage itself. The block often sits in an unresolved plot question.
- **Read.** A good book can restart the writing engine.

## The Hemingway Stop

Stop in the middle of a scene, best of all mid-sentence, while the next move is clear. The next day the entry point is already there: not a blank page but a thread to pull on.

Hemingway put it as a rule: stop when you know what happens next, not when you are exhausted. The trick works because the brain keeps working overnight, and by morning the scene has often been thought through to its end.

One of the most effective anti-block practices there is.

*Source: Ernest Hemingway, A Moveable Feast (1964)*

---

## The Three-Day Rule

If the work has been interrupted for more than three days, read the last section aloud before writing on. Not silently, aloud. Silently the eye reads what it already knows; aloud the ear hears what is actually on the page.

That restores voice, pace and open threads before the next word falls. Sanderson's standard procedure after a break.

---

## Writing Tracker

Keep a word count over weeks: date, words, time of day, context (café, desk, commuter train). After two months patterns show up that no resolution can produce: which time of day is productive, which weekday is weak, which place makes the writing flow.

Trackers are not discipline tools, they are self-observation. Anyone who keeps one as a mandatory quota gives up after three weeks. Anyone who keeps one as a logbook sees his own rhythm.

A simple format is enough: spreadsheet, notebook, markdown file.

## Dictation

By now widespread for long first drafts. Speech recognition is good enough that a raw novel can come into being by voice, while walking, in the car, in bed. Speed is often three to four times that of typed prose.

**Advantages:** The narrative voice sounds more natural because it comes out of the mouth rather than the fingers. Blocks dissolve, because speaking is a different mode.

**Disadvantages:** Raw dictated text needs heavy editing. Repetitions, filler words, broken-off constructions. Dictation does not replace the editing, it moves the work.

**Known practitioners:** Kevin J. Anderson (more than 165 books), Patricia Cornwell.
